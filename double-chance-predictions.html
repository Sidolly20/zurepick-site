How to read Double chance picks (without squinting)

Double chance covers two of the three results (1X, 12 or X2), so it wins more often than a straight 1X2 pick but pays less. It suits matches where you trust one side not to lose but cannot call the winner. Prices marked with ~ are estimated from the 1X2 odds, because double chance prices aren’t always in our feed. The chance shown is calculated from the same odds.

Tap any odds button to drop it into your slip. The glowing price is the market favourite. Once a game ends you’ll see a ✓ or ✗, because a tip you can’t check is just an opinion. The track record has the full 30-day scorecard, and the AI picker can turn today’s best picks into a 2 odds or 3 odds slip in one tap.

When double chance makes sense

Double chance suits games where you trust one side not to lose but cannot call the winner: a good home team against a similar opponent, or a strong visitor away from home. Because it covers two of the three results, the price is short, so we only publish it when the chance is high.

Common mistakes to avoid

People often treat double chance as “safe” and pile five of them into one slip. Each leg still loses sometimes, and short prices multiply into a total that is barely worth the risk. Use it for a single leg you really believe in, not as a way to avoid thinking.

A worked example

If 1X2 odds are 2.10, 3.30 and 3.60, the chances are roughly 45%, 29% and 26% after margin. Home-or-draw is therefore about 74%, which is why a fair price for 1X is near 1.35. When we show a price with a ~ sign, it is worked out this way from the 1X2 odds.

Frequently asked questions

What is double chance?

Double chance covers two of the three results (1X, 12 or X2), so it wins more often than a straight 1X2 pick but pays less. It suits matches where you trust one side not to lose but cannot call the winner.

Are these football predictions really free?

Yes. Every pick, odds table and result is free, and you never need to sign up.

Where do the picks come from?

From the bookmakers’ own prices. We strip out their margin and back the outcome they rate most likely. The percentage is that market-implied chance. It isn’t a secret formula and it isn’t a guarantee.

Why do some games have no pick?

Because a coin flip isn’t a tip. We only publish picks at 60% or more for 1X2, over/under 2.5 and BTTS, and 75% or more for double chance and over 1.5. In our tracked results, picks below that lost far more often, so we leave them out.

What does the percentage mean?

A 70% pick is one the market rates at roughly 70%, so it still loses about three times in ten. Treat it as a gauge of how one-sided a match looks, not a promise.

Are there sure odds or banker tips?

There are strong picks, but no sure ones. Football is built to surprise people. The strongest picks here are the ones the market rates highest, and every result is on the track record page for you to judge.

What does BTTS or GG mean?

BTTS (also called GG) means both teams to score. It wins when each side finds the net at least once, whatever the final result. A 1-1 draw and a 4-3 win both count.

How often does the double chance favourite win?
