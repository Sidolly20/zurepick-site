Betting bankroll and staking: the boring guide that saves money

Nobody posts about this part, but it’s the part that decides whether betting stays fun.

Written by A. S. Jaye, founder and editor · Reviewed 20 November 2026

Set a budget first

Decide an amount you could lose without it touching rent, food or bills, before you place a single bet. Treat it as the price of entertainment, like a cinema ticket.

Keep stakes small

Many careful bettors keep each stake to around 1–2% of their betting budget. That way a cold streak stings but doesn’t end your month.

Never chase

After a loss, the urge to win it back with a bigger bet is the single most expensive habit in betting. If you feel it, close the app.

Know when to stop

If gambling stops being fun, hides from friends or family, or eats money you need, step away and speak to a support service in your country. See our responsible gambling page.

No pick, ours or anyone else’s, is a sure thing.

How to manage a betting bankroll: a short version

Set aside an amount you can afford to lose and treat it as spent. Bet 1% to 2% of it per bet. Never top up after a loss to chase. Write every bet down. After 100 bets you will know more about your real results than any tipster can tell you. Try the stake size calculator to set your unit.

A simple weekly routine

  • Sunday: set the week’s budget, and the unit (1% to 2%).
  • Each day: write the bet, the odds, the stake and the reason, before you place it.
  • After each result: record win or loss. Do not change the stake because of it.
  • Next Sunday: add up the week. If the bankroll changed by a meaningful amount, reset the unit. If not, leave it.

It sounds boring, and that is the point. The people who last in betting are the ones who treat it like bookkeeping.

Frequently asked questions

What is a bankroll?

It’s the amount of money you’ve set aside for betting, separate from money you need for everyday life.

How much should I stake per bet?

There’s no perfect number, but many people keep each stake to a small slice of their budget, around 1–2%, and use less on long-shot slips.

How do I manage my betting bankroll?

Use a fixed bankroll, bet 1% to 2% per bet, never chase losses and keep a record of every bet.
