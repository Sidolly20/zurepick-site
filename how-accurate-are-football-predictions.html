How accurate are football predictions? What a 70% pick really means

Every tips site claims to be accurate. Here is how to test that claim yourself, using nothing but arithmetic.

Accuracy is a range, not a number

Suppose a site publishes 100 picks, and the market rates each one at 70%. You would expect about 70 winners. You would not be shocked by 64 or by 76, because luck swings results around. The usual spread for 100 picks at 70% is about 9 wins either side of 70, so anything from roughly 61 to 79 is ordinary.

Now shrink the sample. With only 10 picks at 70%, a run of 4 winners or 10 winners can both happen without anyone being good or bad at the job. This is why “we won 9 out of our last 10” means very little, and why a site that shows only its best week is telling you nothing.

A high win rate is not the same as profit

A pick priced at 1.30 needs to win about 77% of the time just to break even, because 1 divided by 1.30 is 0.77. If it wins 70% of the time, a 1 stake returns 0.70 × 1.30 = 0.91 on average. You are losing about 9 cents on every unit staked, even though seven bets in ten win.

That is the trap in short-priced tips. They feel accurate, because they usually win, and they still cost money after the bookmaker’s margin. We say this plainly because our own picks are mostly strong favourites. A good win rate on those picks is the expected result, and it is not proof of an edge.

How to judge any tips site

Look for four things. First, are losses shown, or only wins? Second, is every pick recorded before kick-off, or can results be edited afterwards? Third, is the sample large enough, meaning hundreds of picks and not dozens? Fourth, does the site tell you the average odds, so you can work out whether the win rate would have been profitable?

The honest summary

Football predictions can be useful for sorting games into likely and unlikely. They cannot remove the chance of losing. If a site promises 90% or 100% accuracy, it is either hiding losses or selling you something.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good accuracy for football predictions?

It depends on the odds. A site that picks short-priced favourites should win often, but the real test is whether the win rate beats the break-even rate for the average odds.

Can anyone predict football with 90% accuracy?

Not over a meaningful number of games. Football has too much randomness, and claims like that usually come from small samples or hidden losses.

How many predictions do you need to judge a tipster?

Hundreds. With fewer than about 100 picks, luck can easily explain a great or a poor record.

Does a 70% pick always win?

No. It loses roughly three times in ten, and several losses in a row are normal.
