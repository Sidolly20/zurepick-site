Long shot, with receipts

A 5 odds slip is the one with ambition. It takes four or five legs, and every extra leg means one more way to lose. Treat it as a lottery-style ticket with better homework behind it, not as income, and keep the stake smaller than on your 2 or 3 odds slips.

How we build the 5 odds slip

We look at every game with a published price that day, keep the picks the market rates at 60% or more (75% for double chance and over 1.5), then test the combinations and keep the one that lands closest to 5.00 with the best combined chance. Fewer legs usually means a better chance, so we start small and add games only when we have to.

Using it sensibly

Stake an amount you could shrug off. Remember, four 70% picks together land about 24% of the time, which is why every slip shows its combined chance. Want a different mix? Frequently asked questions

Is a 5 odds slip realistic?

It can land, but the maths is firm: four 70% picks together win about 24% of the time. That’s why we show the combined chance and suggest a smaller stake.

Why does the 5 odds slip sometimes show “best available”?

On quiet days there may not be enough strong picks to reach 5.00. Rather than pad it with weak ones, we show the closest slip we can honestly build.

Where can I see how past slips did?

Every result is posted on the track record page, wins and losses alike.

Where do the picks come from?

From the bookmakers’ own prices. We strip out their margin and back the outcome they rate most likely. The percentage is that market-implied chance. It isn’t a secret formula and it isn’t a guarantee.

Why do some games have no pick?

Because a coin flip isn’t a tip. We only publish picks at 60% or more for 1X2, over/under 2.5 and BTTS, and 75% or more for double chance and over 1.5. In our tracked results, picks below that lost far more often, so we leave them out.
