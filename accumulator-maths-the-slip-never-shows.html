The accumulator maths the slip never shows you

Why a slip of safe-looking legs is riskier than it feels, with the real arithmetic for 70%, 80% and 90% legs.

By A. S. Jaye · 12 Nov 2026

Take a leg you are 80% sure about. Add a second one just as sure. The slip now wins about 64% of the time. Add a third and it is 51%. By the sixth leg you are below one in four, and every leg felt safe when you added it. The bars above show it for 80% legs: legs along the bottom, chance of the slip winning on top.

These assume the legs are independent and each is exactly as likely as stated, which is the generous case. Real slips often contain two games from the same weekend, the same league or the same style of pick, and that correlation does not rescue you.

So are accumulators bad?

No. They are a way of turning small edges into a bigger price, and they are fun. They are just a poor way to chase safety. If you like them, keep the legs few, use the highest-confidence picks, and treat the stake as money you can lose. Our 2 odds and 3 odds slips are built that way on purpose, and the calculator shows the maths for any slip you build.
