What a draw pick means here

A draw is almost never the market’s favourite result. The five games with the highest draw chance on a normal day average only about 30%, which is a fair draw price near 3.3. So these are not “likely” draws. They are the games where a draw is relatively most plausible: two evenly matched sides, low expected goals, or both. We take the bookmaker’s draw price, remove the margin and rank games by the chance that is left.

How these picks have done

We replayed the 17 days we hold results for. On each day we took the five games with the highest draw chance. That is 85 games, and 29 ended level (34%). Across every game with prices, 24% ended level (1483 games). A flat stake on each of those draws would have returned +0.4 units. It is a small sample, so read it as a description and not a promise. Draw odds near 3.30 need a draw about 30% of the time just to break even.

Should you back draws?

It is one of the harder bets in football, because the result can look obvious for 85 minutes and still end level. If you like a team but fear a draw, draw no bet refunds your stake on a draw and double chance covers two of the three results. Both pay less. Whatever you choose, keep the stake small and read why no tip is ever sure.

Frequently asked questions

How do you predict a draw?

We take the bookmaker’s draw price, remove the margin and rank games by the chance that is left. Evenly matched teams and low-scoring games rank highest.

How often do football games end in a draw?

In the 9,382 finished games we track, 23% ended level.

What odds do draws usually pay?

For most games between about 3.00 and 4.00. The tightest, lowest-scoring games sit at the shorter end.

Is there a safer way to cover the draw?

Yes. Double chance covers two of the three results, and draw no bet refunds your stake if the game ends level. Both pay less than a straight win.
