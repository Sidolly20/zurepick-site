Value betting explained: when are odds worth taking?

A pick can be likely to win and still be a bad bet. Value is the reason.

The idea

Every price has a break-even point. Odds of 2.50 pay 2.50 for every 1 staked, so you need to win 40% of the time (1 ÷ 2.50) just to get your money back. If the real chance of the outcome is higher than 40%, the bet has value. If it’s lower, it doesn’t, even if the team is “likely” to win.

A worked example

Suppose you think a team has a 45% chance of winning, and the bookmaker offers 2.50. Over many bets of 1 each, you’d expect 0.45 × 2.50 = 1.125 back for every 1 staked. That’s an expected profit of 12.5%. Now suppose the odds were 2.10. Then 0.45 × 2.10 = 0.945, and you’d be expected to lose about 5.5% in the long run. Same team, same belief, a different decision.

Why this is harder than it sounds

The whole thing depends on your number being right. Bookmakers have large teams and a lot of data, and their prices also include a margin, usually a few percent on the main markets. To win in the long run you need to be more accurate than the market, and by more than the margin. A few good weeks don’t prove that you are.

Using the tools on this site

We can’t tell you which prices are bad. What we can do is show you the bookmaker’s implied chance with the margin removed, so you can see the “fair” figure. If you think the real chance is higher than that, that’s a candidate for value. If you’re only repeating the market’s view, there’s none. Our odds guide shows the maths, and the odds converter turns any format into a percentage.

Habits that help

  • Write your estimate down before you look at the odds.
  • Track every bet, wins and losses, for at least a few hundred bets before you trust any conclusion.
  • Stay with small, steady stakes. See bankroll and staking.
  • Be suspicious of any site that says it finds “guaranteed value”.

Frequently asked questions

What is a value bet?

A bet where the odds are higher than the true chance of the outcome justifies. If the real chance is above 1 divided by the odds, the bet has value.

How do I work out break-even?

Divide 1 by the decimal odds. Odds of 2.50 need you to win 40% of the time to break even.

Does a likely winner always have value?

No. A team can be very likely to win and still be priced too short to be worth backing.
