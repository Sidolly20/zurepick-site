When both teams not to score (BTTS No) is worth a look

BTTS No is the clean-sheet bet. It suits a dominant home side against a team that struggles to create chances, or a match where one side is likely to sit back. It also pairs naturally with a win for the favourite, because a 2-0 wins both.

Where it goes wrong

The risk is the consolation goal. A late strike from the underdog turns a comfortable win into a lost bet. Look at how often each team has scored recently, not just the headline price, and treat a team with a tired defence as a warning sign.

A worked example

If Yes is 2.20 and No is 1.65, the raw chances are 45.5% and 60.6%, adding to about 106%. After removing the margin, No is about 57%, under our line. At 2.40 and 1.55 it clears 60% and the game would be listed.

Frequently asked questions

What does both teams not to score (BTTS No) mean?

At least one team fails to score. A game lists when the bookmakers rate that at 60% or better, which usually means a strong favourite against a weak attack, or two cautious sides.

How do you choose both teams not to score (BTTS No) picks?

We take the bookmaker’s odds for the market, remove the margin to get fair chances, and list only the games where this outcome clears our quality bar. Quiet days are normal; an empty list is better than a weak tip.
