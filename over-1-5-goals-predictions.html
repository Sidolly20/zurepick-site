How to read Over/Under 1.5 goals picks (without squinting)

Over 1.5 goals needs at least two goals in the match. It is a lower-risk goals market with shorter odds than Over 2.5, which is why it is a popular accumulator leg.

When over/under 1.5 goals makes sense

Over 1.5 needs just two goals, which most football matches produce, so it is the safest goals line and a popular accumulator leg. It fits games with at least one attacking side, and it is weakest in tight, cagey games between two defensive teams.

Common mistakes to avoid

Because the price is short, Over 1.5 only pays if you are right nearly every time. Stacking many Over 1.5 legs makes a slip that looks safe but loses to one 1-0. Also remember that 0-0 and 1-0 results do happen, particularly in lower leagues and cup ties.

A worked example

Over 1.5 at 1.30 and Under 1.5 at 3.40 imply 76.9% and 29.4%. Normalised, that is about 72% and 28%. If you think the real chance of two or more goals is above 72%, the price has value; if not, skip it.

Frequently asked questions

What is over/under 1.5 goals?

Over 1.5 goals needs at least two goals in the match. It is a lower-risk goals market with shorter odds than Over 2.5, which is why it is a popular accumulator leg.

Are these football predictions really free?

Yes. Every pick, odds table and result is free, and you never need to sign up.

Where do the picks come from?

From the bookmakers’ own prices. We strip out their margin and back the outcome they rate most likely. The percentage is that market-implied chance. It isn’t a secret formula and it isn’t a guarantee.

Why do some games have no pick?

Because a coin flip isn’t a tip. We only publish picks at 60% or more for 1X2, over/under 2.5 and BTTS, and 75% or more for double chance and over 1.5. In our tracked results, picks below that lost far more often, so we leave them out.

What does the percentage mean?

A 70% pick is one the market rates at roughly 70%, so it still loses about three times in ten. Treat it as a gauge of how one-sided a match looks, not a promise.

Are there sure odds or banker tips?

There are strong picks, but no sure ones. Football is built to surprise people. The strongest picks here are the ones the market rates highest, and every result is on the track record page for you to judge.

What does BTTS or GG mean?

BTTS (also called GG) means both teams to score. It wins when each side finds the net at least once, whatever the final result. A 1-1 draw and a 4-3 win both count.

How often does the over/under 1.5 goals favourite win?

Over the last 30 days the favourite won 989 of 1244 tracked matches (80%). That is a hit rate of the market favourite, not a promise for future games.
