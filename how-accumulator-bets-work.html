How accumulator bets work

Accumulators turn small prices into a bigger one. The price you pay is risk.

The maths

Multiply the odds of every leg. Three games at 1.45 give 1.45 × 1.45 × 1.45, about 3.05. Every leg must win, so a single loss sinks the slip.

The chances multiply too

If each leg has a 70% chance, two legs land 49% of the time, three land about 34% and four about 24%. This is why we show a combined chance beside each slip: it’s the honest number.

Picking a size

Fewer legs is nearly always a better chance. That’s why we build our 2 odds, 3 odds and 5 odds slips with the fewest games that can reach the target.

Keep it fun

Use a smaller stake on bigger slips. A 5 odds slip is a ticket, not a plan.

Frequently asked questions

How many legs should an accumulator have?

As few as can reach your target odds. Every extra leg adds another way to lose.

Why did my accumulator lose with only one wrong leg?

Because every leg must win. That’s the trade-off for the bigger combined odds.
