How accumulator bets work

Accumulators turn small prices into a bigger one. The price you pay is risk.

Written by A. S. Jaye, founder and editor · Reviewed 20 November 2026

The maths

Multiply the odds of every leg. Three games at 1.45 give 1.45 × 1.45 × 1.45, about 3.05. Every leg must win, so a single loss sinks the slip.

The chances multiply too

If each leg has a 70% chance, two legs land 49% of the time, three land about 34% and four about 24%. This is why we show a combined chance beside each slip: it’s the honest number.

Picking a size

Fewer legs is nearly always a better chance. That’s why we build our 2 odds, 3 odds and 5 odds slips with the fewest games that can reach the target.

Keep it fun

Use a smaller stake on bigger slips. A 5 odds slip is a ticket, not a plan.

More on staying in control: play responsibly. 18+ only.

A worked example, step by step

Say you stake 1,000 on four legs priced 1.50 each. The total odds are 1.50 × 1.50 × 1.50 × 1.50 = 5.06, so the return is 5,062 and the profit is 4,062. That looks generous until you do the second half of the sum. If each leg wins 66.7% of the time, which is what 1.50 implies, all four win together only about 20% of the time, or one slip in five.

This is why accumulators feel exciting and lose often. The price grows with every leg, but the chance of winning shrinks even faster.

Things that surprise people

  • If one leg is void, for example the match is postponed, most bookmakers settle the slip without it, at odds of 1.00 for that leg. Check the rules in your app.
  • Legs from the same match can be linked. A bookmaker may not allow them in a normal accumulator, which is what a bet builder is for.
  • Cash out lets you take a smaller return before all legs finish. Read cash out explained before relying on it.
  • Systems such as the Lucky 15 and Yankee cover several combinations so one loss does not end the whole bet.
  • Bonuses on accumulators can look big but come with conditions. Read the small print.

Frequently asked questions

How many legs should an accumulator have?

As few as can reach your target odds. Every extra leg adds another way to lose.

Why did my accumulator lose with only one wrong leg?

Because every leg must win. That’s the trade-off for the bigger combined odds.
