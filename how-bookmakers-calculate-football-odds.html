How bookmakers calculate football odds

Odds look mysterious until you see the steps. There are only four.

Written by A. S. Jaye, founder and editor · Reviewed 20 November 2026

Step 1: estimate the chances

Bookmakers use ratings, recent results, injuries, home advantage and models to estimate the chance of each result. Say they think a home win is 50%, a draw 28% and an away win 22%.

Step 2: turn chances into fair odds

Fair decimal odds are 1 divided by the chance: 2.00, 3.57 and 4.55.

Step 3: add the margin

The bookmaker shaves the odds so the implied chances add to more than 100%. The prices might become 1.90, 3.40 and 4.20. Their implied chances are 52.6%, 29.4% and 23.8%, which total 105.9%. The extra 5.9% is the margin.

Step 4: adjust to the money

If lots of bets come in on one side, the price on that side shortens and the other side lengthens. That is why odds move before kick-off and why a price at breakfast may not be a price at lunch.

What this means for you

The price tells you the bookmaker’s opinion plus their cut. ZurePicks removes the cut to show the chance, so you can compare picks fairly. Try it yourself with the margin remover or the odds converter, and read margin and overround explained for more.

A worked example for goals markets

Two-way markets work the same way. Say a bookmaker offers Over 2.5 at 1.85 and Under 2.5 at 1.95. The implied chances are 54.1% and 51.3%, which add to 105.3%. The extra 5.3% is the margin. Remove it by dividing each by 1.053 and you get a fair 51.3% for over and 48.7% for under. Notice that the bookmaker does not think over is 54%. It thinks it is about 51%, and charges you for the difference.

Why two bookmakers can disagree

Bookmakers do not copy each other exactly. They use different models, different customers and different risk. A bookmaker whose customers love backing home teams will shorten the away price to protect itself, while another does the opposite. That is why the same game can be 1.85 in one app and 1.78 in another, and why it can pay to compare before you confirm.

Margins are not the same everywhere

Big leagues with lots of money traded on them, like the Premier League, usually have tighter margins. Smaller leagues have wider ones because the bookmaker knows less and risks more. Accumulators multiply the margin on every leg, so a slip of five legs from small leagues can carry a very large hidden cost. The accumulator maths guide shows the numbers.

What “value” means

A bet has value when the real chance of winning is higher than the chance the price implies. If you believe a team wins 55% of the time and the odds are 2.10, which implies 47.6%, you see value. The hard part is that your estimate of 55% might be wrong. That is why most professionals stake small and why expected value is a long-run idea, not a promise on any single bet.

Frequently asked questions

How do bookmakers calculate football odds?

They estimate each result’s chance, convert it into odds, add a margin and adjust for the money placed.

Why do odds change before a match?

Because of team news and because bookmakers move prices to balance the bets they receive.
