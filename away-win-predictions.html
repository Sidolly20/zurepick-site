When away win is worth a look

An away favourite is a statement. The bookmaker has already given the home side its crowd, its pitch and its rest, and still rates the visitors above 60%. That usually means a big gap in quality: a league leader travelling to a promoted side, or a cup tie between a top club and a minnow. Away prices are also often longer than home prices for the same chance, which is why some people look here first.

Where it goes wrong

Do not assume every long-priced away side is value. A price drifting out before kick-off can mean the market knows about a missing player. And watch the schedule: a team playing its third game in a week can look great on paper and run out of legs in the second half.

A worked example

Suppose a visiting side is priced at 1.62, with the draw at 4.00 and the home side at 5.50. After removing the margin, the away win comes out near 59%, just under our line, so it would not be listed. If the same game were 1.50 away, 4.20 draw and 7.00 home, the away chance would rise to about 64% and it would appear. A small price change can move a game across the bar, which is the point of having one.

Frequently asked questions

What does away win mean?

Visiting teams the bookmakers rate as clear favourites. Backing a side away from home is a harder sell, so these only show up when the market is unusually sure.

How do you choose away win picks?

We take the bookmaker’s odds for the market, remove the margin to get fair chances, and list only the games where this outcome clears our quality bar. Quiet days are normal; an empty list is better than a weak tip.

Is this guaranteed?

No. A 70% pick still loses three times in ten. Every result, win or lose, goes on the track record page.
