How to read Match result (1X2) picks (without squinting)

The 1X2 market is the classic match-result bet: 1 is a home win, X is a draw and 2 is an away win. Every pick below is the outcome the bookmaker prices as most likely, with the bookmaker margin removed, so the percentage is the market’s own implied chance.

Tap any odds button to drop it into your slip. The glowing price is the market favourite. Once a game ends you’ll see a ✓ or ✗, because a tip you can’t check is just an opinion. The track record has the full 30-day scorecard, and the AI picker can turn today’s best picks into a 2 odds or 3 odds slip in one tap.

When match result (1x2) makes sense

A 1X2 pick makes sense when one side is clearly better and the price still pays. Think of a strong home team against a side near the bottom of the table, a team chasing a title against one with nothing to play for, or a visitor who is simply much stronger on paper. It is a poor fit for evenly matched games, where the draw is a real third outcome that eats into every price.

Common mistakes to avoid

The most common mistake is backing a short-priced favourite in every game. At odds of 1.20 you need to win about five times out of six just to break even, and no market is that reliable week after week. The second is forgetting the draw: a team rated at 60% still fails to win four games in ten.

A worked example

Say home is 1.80, draw 3.60 and away 4.80. Divide 1 by each price (0.556, 0.278, 0.208) and the total is 1.042, so the bookmaker is keeping about 4% as margin. Divide each by 1.042 and the market’s own chances become 53%, 27% and 20%. That is the figure we show.

Frequently asked questions

What is match result (1x2)?

The 1X2 market is the classic match-result bet: 1 is a home win, X is a draw and 2 is an away win. Every pick below is the outcome the bookmaker prices as most likely, with the bookmaker margin removed, so the percentage is the market’s own implied chance.

Are these football predictions really free?

Yes. Every pick, odds table and result is free, and you never need to sign up.

Where do the picks come from?

From the bookmakers’ own prices. We strip out their margin and back the outcome they rate most likely. The percentage is that market-implied chance. It isn’t a secret formula and it isn’t a guarantee.

Why do some games have no pick?

Because a coin flip isn’t a tip. We only publish picks at 60% or more for 1X2, over/under 2.5 and BTTS, and 75% or more for double chance and over 1.5. In our tracked results, picks below that lost far more often, so we leave them out.

What does the percentage mean?

A 70% pick is one the market rates at roughly 70%, so it still loses about three times in ten. Treat it as a gauge of how one-sided a match looks, not a promise.

Are there sure odds or banker tips?

There are strong picks, but no sure ones. Football is built to surprise people. The strongest picks here are the ones the market rates highest, and every result is on the track record page for you to judge.

What does BTTS or GG mean?

BTTS (also called GG) means both teams to score. It wins when each side finds the net at least once, whatever the final result. A 1-1 draw and a 4-3 win both count.

How often does the match result (1x2) favourite win?

Over the last 30 days the favourite won 847 of 1538 tracked matches (55%). That is a hit rate of the market favourite, not a promise for future games.
