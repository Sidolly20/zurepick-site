What is a correct score bet? Odds, chances and the catch

Name the exact score and the price is tempting. Here is what the numbers behind it look like.

How it works

You pick the exact final score after 90 minutes plus stoppage time, for example 2-1. If the game ends 2-1 you win. Any other score loses. Extra time and penalties do not count in most bookmakers’ rules, so check the small print for cup games.

How likely is any one score?

Not very. Take a typical home favourite, where the home side is expected to score about 1.5 goals and the away side about 1.1. A simple goals model (a Poisson model, the standard starting point for football scores) gives these chances:

ScoreChanceFair odds
1-112.3%8.2
1-011.1%9.0
2-19.2%10.9
2-08.4%12.0
0-18.2%12.2
0-07.4%13.5

Even the likeliest score lands only about one time in eight, and the five likeliest together cover about 49% of outcomes. The other half is spread over dozens of scores.

What real games look like

In the 9,391 finished games we have tracked, the most common scorelines were 1-1 (12%), 1-0 (9%), 2-1 (9%), 1-2 (7%) and 0-1 (7%). The same few scores keep turning up, but none of them is close to a coin flip.

The catch: the margin

Fair odds are 1 divided by the chance. A score with a 12.3% chance has fair odds of 8.2. Bookmakers shave that price, and on correct score they shave much more than on a match result. A margin of 15% or more is common here, against roughly 5% to 10% on 1X2. If a bookmaker offers 7.00 on that 12.3% score, you need to win 14.3% of the time (1 ÷ 7.00) just to break even, so you lose money over time even if your reading of the game is right.

Is it ever sensible?

Mostly as entertainment, with a small stake you would not miss. It is a poor accumulator leg, because each leg wins about one time in ten. We do not publish correct score picks, because our odds feed has no correct score prices, and a guess with no price is not a pick. To judge any price yourself, read how to read football odds and value betting explained.

Frequently asked questions

What is a correct score bet?

A bet on the exact final score of a match, for example 2-1. It wins only if the game ends with that exact score after 90 minutes plus stoppage time.

How likely is the most common correct score?

For a typical home favourite the likeliest score, often 1-1 or 1-0, has about a 11% to 12% chance, which is roughly one game in eight or nine.

Why are correct score odds so high?

Because there are many possible scores and each one is unlikely. The bookmaker’s margin is also usually bigger than on a match result.

Is correct score a good bet?

It is a high-risk, high-margin market. Most people treat it as entertainment with a small stake and never put it in an accumulator.
