What “single shot” means on ZurePicks

It is one game and one outcome, bet on its own. No stacking, no “just add one more leg”. Every day we name a shot of the day and a backup, and that is the whole list. We hold the line at two games because a longer list stops being a pick and turns into a menu.

The price is the other fixed point. We aim at 2.00, so a winning 100 stake comes back as 200. If you want more return than that, the honest route is the 3 odds slip, not a longer single price. Higher single prices mean a lower chance, and the chance is the part we care about.

How the pick is chosen

For today we have 35 games with prices. Tomorrow has 97 so far, and that number grows as bookmakers publish more. For each game that has not kicked off we start with the match result, and only a Home win or an Away win can be picked, never a draw. A win pick must also be clearly more likely than the other side, so coin-flip games are skipped. Goals markets such as Over and Under are only a fill-in on days when too few win picks exist, and never more than one of them a day.

Bookmaker prices carry a margin, so the raw odds overstate every outcome a little. We strip that margin out, which leaves the chance the market really believes in. Then we keep only outcomes priced between 1.90 and 2.20. Only if fewer than two games qualify do we widen that to 1.85 to 2.30. Of what is left, the highest chance wins, one outcome per game. Prices that look wrong, such as a missing draw price or an odd margin, are ignored. Right now the shot of the day is Latina v Campobasso FC, Home win at 2.06, which the market puts at 44%.

Why the chance is only around 50%

This surprises people, so it is worth saying plainly. Odds of 2.00 mean the market thinks the outcome happens about half the time. After the bookmaker’s margin, the best you can find near that price is usually 48 to 52%. A single at 2.00 is a close call by construction. What we do is find the best-priced close call of the day, not a safe one. If you want safer, use 2 odds built from shorter-priced games, and accept that a 70% leg still loses three times in ten.

How the shot has done so far

We replayed every day we hold results for. That is 19 days, using the pre-match prices saved for each one. The shot of the day won 11 of 19 (58%) and the backup won 12 of 19 (63%). At a flat stake on the shot of the day that comes to +2.1 units. It is a small sample and we are not hiding behind that, because a few more weeks could move it either way. Prices near 2.00 are a coin flip with a margin attached, and any honest record will look like this for a while.

Single or accumulator?

Take two games with a 71% chance each. Put them in one slip and the price comes to about 2.0, but you need both to land, and the combined chance is 50%. A single at 2.00 also sits near 50%. So the chance is about the same, and what changes is the way you can lose. In the slip, either game can sink you. In the single, there is only one game to watch. If your problem is a ticket that dies at 89 minutes in the fourth leg, the single removes it. The accumulators guide goes through the arithmetic.

Using it without getting hurt

Decide the stake before you open the page, and make it an amount you could lose without it mattering. Do not chase a loss with a bigger single the next day. The shot of the day is not a signal to bet more. 18+ only. If gambling is starting to feel like a problem, get help here.

Frequently asked questions

What is a single bet in football?

A bet on one outcome of one game, settled on its own. There is no accumulator and no other leg that can affect it. If your pick wins, you are paid at its price.

How is the single shot picked?

For each game that has not started we take the Home win and the Away win, remove the bookmaker’s margin, keep outcomes priced between 1.90 and 2.20 that are clearly ahead of the other side, and take the one with the highest chance. Goals markets only fill in when too few win picks exist. There is one outcome per game, and a backup from a different game.

Why is the chance only about 50%?

Because the price is about 2.00. Odds of 2.00 mean the market sees the outcome as roughly a coin flip. After the bookmaker’s margin, the best price in that range is usually a 48 to 52% chance.

Can I get a single at 3 odds?

We keep the single shot at around 2.00 on purpose. A single at 3.00 has a chance nearer 33%, so it wins about one time in three. If you want 3 odds, the 3 odds slip spreads the risk across several shorter-priced games.

Is the single shot a sure win?

No. Nothing is. A pick near 2.00 loses about as often as it wins, and our own replay shows the record in full, wins and losses.

When does the shot change?

Whenever the prices move. The page is rebuilt through the day, so a game can drop out as its odds drift, and tomorrow’s shot firms up as more games are priced.
