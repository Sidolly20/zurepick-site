When home or draw (1X) is worth a look

1X is the careful version of a home-win bet. You give up some of the price in return for the draw, which is useful in the games where you rate the home team but suspect it will not score enough to win. It is also a favourite leg for accumulators, because each leg lands more often than a straight win.

Where it goes wrong

The prices are short, often between 1.10 and 1.30, and that is where people go wrong: stacking six of them and expecting the slip to behave like a safe bet. It does not. Six legs at 85% each combine to roughly a 38% slip. Also check that you are not paying double chance prices for a game where the straight home win is almost as likely and pays much more.

A worked example

A home side priced 2.10 to win with the draw at 3.30 and the away side at 3.60 has 1X implied near 74%, so it would not make this list. Move the home price to 1.65, the draw to 3.90 and the away side to 5.20, and 1X rises to about 82%. That game is listed, at a short price of roughly 1.16.

Frequently asked questions

What does home or draw (1X) mean?

The home team wins or the game is a draw. That covers two results out of three, so a pick only appears here when the bookmakers rate the home side at 75% or more not to lose.

How do you choose home or draw (1X) picks?

We take the bookmaker’s odds for the market, remove the margin to get fair chances, and list only the games where this outcome clears our quality bar. Quiet days are normal; an empty list is better than a weak tip.

Is this guaranteed?

No. A 70% pick still loses three times in ten. Every result, win or lose, goes on the track record page.
