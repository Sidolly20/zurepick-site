Bookmaker margin (overround) explained with real numbers

Every set of odds includes a built-in fee. Learn how to see it, measure it and use it to pick better prices.

Written by A. S. Jaye, founder and editor · Reviewed 20 November 2026

The short answer

Turn the odds of every outcome into a chance by dividing 1 by the odds, then add them up. If the total is above 100%, the extra is the bookmaker’s margin, also called the overround. It is how bookmakers earn money whichever team wins.

Worked example: a three-way market

Home 2.10, draw 3.40, away 3.60. The chances are 47.6%, 29.4% and 27.8%. They total 104.8%, so the margin is 4.8%. To get the fair chances, divide each by 1.048: about 45.4%, 28.1% and 26.5%. The fair odds, without the margin, are around 2.20, 3.56 and 3.77.

Worked example: a two-way market

Over 2.5 at 1.91 and under 2.5 at 1.91. Each is 52.4%, so the total is 104.7%. A fair market would be 2.00 each. The 0.09 missing on each side is what you pay.

Why the margin matters to you

The margin is a cost on every bet. A lower margin means better value for the same outcome. Two bookmakers can both offer a home win, but the one with the higher odds on every outcome has the lower margin. Margins also tend to be bigger in smaller leagues and in complicated markets than in major matches.

How we use it

On ZurePick, the chances shown next to our picks are worked out after the margin is removed, which is why they differ from the raw 1 ÷ odds figure. Try it with your own numbers in the margin remover, and see how to read football odds for the basics.

A quick way to compare two bookmakers

Take the same match at two sites and add up the chances for every outcome at each. The lower total has the lower margin. Do this on a few matches and you will see which site is usually cheaper. A site that looks better on one outcome may be worse overall, so compare the whole market, not one price.

Common margin mistakes

  • Reading 1 ÷ odds as the real chance. It includes the margin.
  • Ignoring margin on “boosted” or combined markets, which can be higher.
  • Assuming a low margin means a good bet. It only means a fair price, not a winning pick.

How bookmakers calculate football odds

Bookmakers start with their own estimate of each outcome, adjust for the money coming in on each side, then add a margin so the prices total more than 100%. Read the long version in how bookmakers calculate football odds.

Frequently asked questions

What is the overround?

The amount by which the implied chances of all outcomes exceed 100%. It represents the bookmaker’s built-in fee.

How do you calculate a bookmaker’s margin?

Divide 1 by each outcome’s odds, add the results and subtract 100%. For example 104.8% means a 4.8% margin.

Is a lower margin always better for me?

Yes, for the same outcome a lower margin means higher odds, so you are paid more when you win.

Are fair odds the true odds?

They are an estimate of what the market thinks without the margin. They do not predict the result.
