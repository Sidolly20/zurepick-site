Is this betting site safe? How to check before you deposit

Before you put money into any betting site, spend five minutes on these checks. They stop most of the common scams.

Written by A. S. Jaye, founder and editor · Reviewed 20 November 2026

1. Look for a licence, then verify it

Licensed sites usually show a licence number and the regulator’s name in the footer. Do not trust the badge alone. Open the regulator’s own website, from a search you made yourself, and look the operator up. Laws and regulators differ by country, so check the rules where you live.

2. Check the address carefully

Fake sites copy real ones with a small change in the web address. Type the address yourself or use the official app from your phone’s store, and avoid links sent by strangers on social media or messaging apps.

3. Read the withdrawal rules before you deposit

Check the minimum withdrawal, any fees, how long payouts take and what documents you must show. Be cautious if the rules are hard to find.

4. Understand the bonus terms

A bonus usually comes with a rollover, meaning you must bet a multiple of the bonus before withdrawing. Read the rollover, the minimum odds and the time limit. A big bonus with strict terms is often not worth it.

5. Be careful how you pay

Be wary of sites that ask you to send money to a personal account, or that push crypto only with no working support. A real operator takes payments through proper payment channels.

6. Never pay for tips or “fixed” matches

No one can sell a guaranteed result. Paid “VIP” groups and “fixed match” offers are scams. See how to spot the scam. Never share your password, PIN or one-time codes with anyone, including people who claim to be support.

7. Use the safety tools

Licensed operators offer deposit limits, time-outs and self-exclusion. Set a limit before your first deposit, and read our responsible gambling page and bankroll and staking.

If you have been scammed

Stop paying, as scammers often ask for more to “release” your money. Save screenshots, messages and payment records, contact your bank or mobile money provider, and report it to the regulator or the police in your country.

Frequently asked questions

How can I tell if a betting site is legit?

Find its licence number, then confirm it on the regulator’s own website. Also check that you are on the real web address and read the withdrawal rules.

Is it safe to pay for betting tips?

No. No one can guarantee results, and paid tips and fixed match offers are common scams.

What should I do if a betting site will not pay me?

Keep all records, contact the operator’s support in writing, then the regulator that licensed it. If it is a scam, contact your bank or mobile money provider and the police.

Is betting legal where I live?

It depends on the country and sometimes the region. Check your national or local law, and only bet if you are of legal age.
